BEHIND THE GRAPH

The model, without the mystery.

01 / Elasticity isn’t slope

Elasticity measures percentage responsiveness. On a linear curve it changes as price and quantity change. The sliders set absolute demand elasticity and supply elasticity at E₀ = (100, $50), not along the entire curve.

02 / The equations

With signed policy wedge w (positive for tax, negative for subsidy): Q₁ = (a − c − w) / (b + d), Pᵦ = a − bQ₁, and Pₛ = c + dQ₁.

03 / Welfare assumptions

Perfect competition, no externalities, no administrative costs, and no government purchases of unsold goods. Taxes and subsidies are per unit. For binding price controls, traded quantity is the smaller of quantity demanded and supplied. We assume efficient rationing: the highest-value buyers and lowest-cost sellers trade. Random rationing or search costs can cause additional welfare losses. Subsidy spending is a social cost, so private surplus can rise while total social surplus falls. Government balance is negative for a subsidy.
